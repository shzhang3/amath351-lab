Your matched points satisfy this same integral condition. Small manual matching errors may leave them slightly off the exact curve.

Why does separating variables work?

For y′ = f(t)g(y), on an interval where g(y) ≠ 0, define H(y) by H′(y) = 1/g(y). The chain rule gives dH(y(t))/dt = y′/g(y) = f(t). Integrating from the initial time makes the two accumulated quantities equal.

Here y stays positive, so H(y) = ln y. Thus d(ln y)/dt = y′/y = t. The two shaded areas show the definite integrals, including the initial condition.

Check equilibrium solutions before dividing. The equation y′ = ty also has the solution y = 0. Division by y omits it, although it does not satisfy this example’s initial condition y(0) = 1.

Back to finite-time blowup

For y′ = y² with y(0) = 1, separation gives t = ∫₁ʸ u⁻² du = 1 − 1/y. The total area from 1 to infinity is only 1. The state can go arbitrarily far while the elapsed time approaches a finite limit.
