Section 1.2 · Problem 33

Two drops on Planet Gzyx

A ball released from rest falls 20 feet in 2 seconds. Use that observation to infer the planet's gravity, then predict the fall time and impact speed for a 200-foot drop.

Make a prediction: if the height is ten times larger, is the fall time also ten times larger? Release both balls to compare, then change the second height and try again.

Assume constant gravity and no air resistance. Both drops use the same height and time scales; the balls stop at the ground. Impact speed is measured immediately before contact.

Planet Gzyx: two drops, one gravity

Observed drop: 20 ft in 2 sGravity: 10 ft/s²
20 ft drop
200 ft drop
Shared height scale · upward is positive · trail every 1 s
Infer gravity and explain the scaling

Taking upward as positive gives constant acceleration −g. With release height H and initial velocity zero, integrating twice gives:

v(t) = −gt,   h(t) = H − ½gt².

The observed fall gives 20 = ½g(2²), so g = 10 ft/s². Setting the height to zero gives the fall time; the impact speed is the magnitude of the downward velocity.

T = √(2H/g),   impact speed = gT = √(2gH).

For H = 200 ft, T = √40 ≈ 6.32 s, and the impact speed is 10√40 ≈ 63.25 ft/s. Multiplying height by 10 multiplies both fall time and impact speed by √10, not by 10.

The free-fall equations apply up to first contact. The visualization stops each ball there; it does not model the collision or a bounce.
