Section 3.1 · Dependence on an interval

One multiplier, one whole interval

f(x) = x³,   g(x) = x²|x| = |x|³

Are these functions linearly dependent or independent on ℝ, (0, ∞), and (−∞, 0)? Look for one constant k such that g(x) = kf(x) throughout the selected interval.

Do the functions overlap?

g(x)kf(x)

Can one k match the ratio?

g(x)/f(x), x ≠ 0k

Open circles: the ratio is undefined at x = 0 because f(0) = g(0) = 0.

Reveal the conclusion for this interval

A relation must hold for every x in the interval, with the same constants. The finite graph suggests a pattern; these identities establish it on the entire interval.

But the Wronskian is zero everywhere. Why?

f′(x) = 3x²,   g′(x) = 3x|x|

W(x) = fg′ − f′g = 3x⁴|x| − 3x⁴|x| = 0

On ℝ, these two functions are linearly independent even though W ≡ 0. For arbitrary differentiable functions, a zero Wronskian is not enough to conclude dependence.

The stronger test applies to two solutions of the same homogeneous linear equation y″ + p(x)y′ + q(x)y = 0, with p and q continuous on the whole interval. Under these hypotheses, W(x₀) = 0 at one point implies dependence on that interval.

Those hypotheses fail here across x = 0. Both functions have value and derivative zero at 0 but are not identically zero; they cannot solve such a regular equation across 0, because uniqueness would force the zero solution.

Compare with two solutions of y″ − y = 0, where the Wronskian test does apply.
