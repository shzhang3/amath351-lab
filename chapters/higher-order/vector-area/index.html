Height is the absolute value of s times the sine of the relative angle. The signed area changes sign with orientation; the geometric area is nonnegative.

Why is the height s |sin(β − α)|?

Both α and β are measured from the same horizontal direction. Rotating the picture by −α makes the base horizontal, and v now makes angle θ = β − α with that direction.

For an acute positive θ, the right triangle OBH has hypotenuse OB = s and opposite side BH = h. By definition, sin θ = h/s, so h = s sin θ.

For an obtuse angle, H lies behind O. The small triangle uses the acute angle φ = 180° − |θ|, whose sine equals |sin θ|. For a negative θ, B lies on the other side of the base, while the height remains a nonnegative length. In every case, h = s |sin θ|.

How does this become |ad − bc|?

a = r cos α,   b = r sin α,   c = s cos β,   d = s sin β.

ad − bc = r s (cos α sin β − sin α cos β) = r s sin(β − α).

Taking the absolute value gives the area r h. The sign records orientation. Parallel vectors have zero height and zero area.

For the initial-data arrows (y₁, y₁′) and (y₂, y₂′), this expression is the Wronskian W = y₁y₂′ − y₁′y₂. The geometric area is |W|.
