Can positive acceleration slow you down?

A particle starts at position 1 with velocity −1. Its acceleration stays positive. Predict its initial direction and whether its speed increases or decreases, then play the motion.

a(t) = 1 / √(t + 4),   x(0) = 1,   v(0) = −1.

Move the time slider to compare all three curves. The dashed tangents show the slope of the position and velocity graphs; Turning point jumps to the instant the particle reverses direction.

Connect the motion to the mathematics

Integrate the acceleration and use the initial velocity to determine the first constant. Integrate once more and use the initial position to determine the second.

v(t) = 2√(t + 4) − 5
x(t) = 1 + (4/3)[(t + 4)3/2 − 8] − 5t

Before t = 2.25, velocity is negative and acceleration is positive: the particle moves left while slowing down. At t = 2.25, velocity is zero, position is −1/12, and acceleration is 0.4. It then moves right and speeds up.

Position tells you where the particle is; velocity tells you its direction and rate of motion. Speed is |v|. The sign of acceleration alone does not tell you whether speed is increasing.

The example does not specify physical units. The axes use consistent position and time units. Curves and motion use the exact solution, for t ≥ 0.
