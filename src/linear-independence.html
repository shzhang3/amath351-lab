Connect the geometry to the Wronskian

At x = 0, each solution gives an arrow (yᵢ(0), yᵢ′(0)). Its two coordinates are the starting value and slope. Combining solutions combines these arrows with the same coefficients.

W(0) = y₁(0)y₂′(0) − y₁′(0)y₂(0)

The parallelogram formed by the two unscaled arrows has area |W(0)|. For eˣ and 2eˣ, the arrows lie on one line: changing two coefficients still gives only y = (c₁ + 2c₂)eˣ. For eˣ and e⁻ˣ, W(0) = −2, so the arrows span the plane. With unrestricted real coefficients, every initial value–slope pair has exactly one coefficient pair.

The sliders cover −1 ≤ c₁, c₂ ≤ 1; the independent pair can reach every target offered here within that range. An unreachable target for the dependent pair does not mean that the ODE has no solution. It means that this pair cannot represent that solution.

When does the Wronskian test apply?

For two solutions of the same equation y″ + p(x)y′ + q(x)y = 0, with p and q continuous on an interval I, the solutions are linearly independent on I if and only if W(x₀) ≠ 0 at one point x₀ in I. In this setting, W is either zero everywhere or nonzero everywhere on I.

For arbitrary differentiable functions, W ≡ 0 alone does not prove dependence. Explore x³ and x²|x| on three intervals to see why the hypotheses matter.
