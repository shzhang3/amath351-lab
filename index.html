Lecture 01 · From fluids to differential equations

How far can a rule take us?

A swirling fluid. A moving particle. A simple question: if we know how something changes, can we predict its future?

A question from current research. On September 8, 2026, OpenAI reported a Navier–Stokes construction with smooth external forcing: velocity becomes unbounded in finite time while total kinetic energy stays bounded.

Teaching model. This smooth, incompressible local field illustrates particle motion. It has no finite-time singularity and does not reproduce the paper’s Navier–Stokes solution. The view is a fixed 3D projection.

See the rule behind the motion
x′ = −ax − ωy   ·   y′ = ωx − ay   ·   z′ = 2az
a = 0.24,   ω = rotation rate

The divergence is −a − a + 2a = 0: contraction in the horizontal plane is balanced by vertical stretching. The displayed trajectories are evaluated exactly.

Navier–Stokes describes the whole velocity field through a PDE. Once a field is given, tracking a particle is an ODE problem. We will return to systems like this later in the course.

A separate ODE example. This demonstrates the meaning of finite-time blowup. It is not a reduction of the Navier–Stokes construction. The plot stops at y = 8; the solution does not.

Reveal the exact solution
y(t) = 1 / (1 − t),   t < 1.

Differentiate to check y′ = y², then check y(0) = 1. As t approaches 1 from below, y grows without bound. There is no finite value at t = 1 through which this solution can continue.

The formula also gives values for t > 1, but that disconnected branch is not a continuation of the solution starting at t = 0.

Does a smooth rule last forever?

The rule y′ = y² is smooth everywhere. Predict whether the solution can reach every future time.
